Matchmaking Algorithm Outcomes and Player Engagement in League of Legends

An empirical study examining how winning and losing streaks affect player engagement behavior in competitive MOBA games, using large-scale ranked match data.

Ziyang (Chane) Zhang, Ph.D. Department of Systems & Operations Management David Nazarian College of Business and Economics, CSUN

Research Overview

Matchmaking systems in competitive online games determine which players face each other in every match. Beyond skill-balancing, these systems may inadvertently shape sequences of wins and losses — streaks — that have downstream effects on player motivation and continued engagement.

This study uses large-scale observational data from League of Legends ranked Solo/Duo queues (North America server) to empirically examine how streak patterns relate to player engagement outcomes, including session continuation, match frequency, and behavioral indicators of motivation.

Existing research on matchmaking and engagement either relies on simulation-based validation (Huang et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2026) or tests system-level effects without isolating specific behavioral mechanisms (Fan et al., 2024). This study addresses that gap by linking observable match sequences to engagement behavior through a rank-progression framework.

Data Ethics & Privacy

  • No personally identifiable information (PII) is stored or published.
  • All data is collected through Riot Games' official public API under their Terms of Service.
  • Player identifiers (PUUID, summoner ID) are pseudonymized prior to analysis.
  • Data is used solely for academic research and will not be sold or shared commercially.
  • Findings will be reported only in aggregate form in peer-reviewed academic publications.
  • No attempt is made to re-identify individual players from published results.
